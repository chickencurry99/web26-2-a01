Yankees

MINNESOTA — Even though the results weren’t there, the Yankees kept saying that they liked the way Aaron Judge has looked since returning from a fractured rib on Sept. 8.
“I’ve actually been pleased,” Aaron Boone said before Monday’s series-opener against the Twins.
“Even right away, I felt like he’s looked better than I anticipated with him having not played a lot. He’s just missed a handful of pitches, but I feel like, for the most part,
he’s swinging at the right pitches, taking pitches that he should, not chasing a lot. So I think it’s just a matter of that last level of fine-tuning that we know he’ll get to.
But I feel like it’s been valuable having him have the number of at-bats that he’s had, seeing the number of pitches that he has, playing as regularly as he has. “So [that’s] all hopefully moving towards getting him really dialed in.” Sure enough, Judge looked dialed in during the eighth inning on Monday, pulling a 397-foot, three-run bomb into the second deck at Target Field.
The monstrous blast was Judge’s first since coming off the injured list, and it gave the Yankees plenty of breathing room in an 8-3 win that, along with a Blue Jays loss, secured a playoff berth. José Caballero, who had a solo homer and an RBI single, and Ben Rice, who blooped a go-ahead, two-run single, also helped the Yankees overcome a few defensive blunders.
Austin Wells added a solo homer of his own in the ninth before the Bombers, looking to end a championship drought that dates back to 2009, celebrated their return to the postseason.
Prior to Judge’s home run, he had been 0-for-3 with two strikeouts on Monday. He entered the contest 3-for-17 (.176) with zero extra-base hits in his first five games since coming off the shelf.
The back-to-back MVP, who did not go on a rehab assignment and only had one live batting practice session against minor leaguers before being activated, also walked once and struck out nine times over that stretch.
Judge’s timing had clearly been a bit off, but Boone maintained that results would come with more at-bats.
Boone has also said that he’s been “pleased” with the way Judge has been responding physically since returning to game action. Judge, who has said he wants to be playing every day, said much of the same on Sunday.
“I’m feeling great,” Judge said. “That’s why we’re back. I’m feeling 100 percent, ready to go. I’m feeling really good. We keep winning ballgames, which is the most important thing.”
Boone previously considered giving Judge the day off on Monday, as the right fielder has only missed one game since returning from an injury that sidelined him from May 31 to Sept. 8. With that in mind, Boone said that Judge could sit out Wednesday’s series finale in Minnesota, which comes before an off day,
he “pretty much” sees the slugger as full-go with the Yankees trying to leapfrog the Rays for a division title with two weeks to go before the playoffs.
Tampa Bay, off on Monday, now has a 3.5-game lead over the Yankees in the American League East. According to FanGraphs, the pinstripers had just a 9.6% chance of winning the division at the beginning of the day.

Mets

Famous (fictional) Baltimorean Omar Little once said, if you come at the king, you best not miss.
David Stearns missed when he decided to let the Mets’ home run king walk to Baltimore in free agency.
The Mets’ president of baseball operations missed big. Never has that been more evident than it was Monday night when the “Polar Bear” returned to Citi Field as a member of the Orioles.
The chants of “Pete A-lonso” started during batting practice and picked up again when he came out onto the field to warm up.
Never mind that the Mets had an on-field awards presentation for their minor league players and coaches of the year, and never mind that young adult author Rebecca Yarros threw out the first pitch.
They only quieted when the Mets played a video on the scoreboard recapping Alonso’s best moments. Then the chants turned to “Fire Stearns.”
Alonso was a homegrown stud who endeared himself to the fanbase and made himself a part of the community.
He became one of the most popular players in Mets history, easily in the top-10, or even the top-5.
Alonso was a draw, regularly putting butts in the seats. No offense to Jorge Polanco, Brett Baty, Mark Vientos,
Jared Young or anyone else who has played first base for the Mets this season, but they don’t command the same fan attention as Alonso, and likely never will.
What other visiting player has ever received standing ovations in each at-bat? A phenomenal steward of the organization,
a player who could produce in the biggest moments on baseball’s biggest stage, a beloved clubhouse character and someone who plays 162 games a year,
Alonso isn’t the type of player you let walk without bothering to even try.
Babe Ruth started his baseball career in Baltimore and became a star in New York.
Alonso took the opposite approach, going to Baltimore well after his star power
and his home-run swing had been established in New York.
The Ruthian road is still the more common one traveled by baseball’s biggest stars, since small-market teams like the Orioles develop talent but can’t afford to retain it.
The Mets have the luxury of being a large-market team with a large budget, one that can afford to retain their homegrown talent.
The Orioles only wish they could retain the players they developed into All-Stars. Stearns should understand this as the former POBO of the Milwaukee Brewers,
a team that annually trades away its homegrown stars. But it’s difficult to know what Stearns was thinking when it came to Alonso, and whether he regrets the decision to move on from him after eight seasons in a Mets uniform.
He has declined to comment each time he’s been asked about Alonso, a player drafted several regimes ago.
Those who are familiar with his decision-making have said that the Mets were concerned about his age and the natural decline that comes with players over 30.
The projections Stearns had didn’t look good. The Mets saw a middle-lineup stud who was already declining offensively and quickly declining defensively.
They didn’t think he could get any better at his age.
Alonso proved them wrong by improving nearly every aspect of his game in Baltimore this season.
Not for nothing, but the Mets were unconcerned with how Juan Soto would age when they gave him a contract that runs until he’s 40, and he was never a plus defender.
The numbers don’t tell the entire story. They don’t take into account the influence he had in the clubhouse and the entertainment value he provided.
Sandy Alderson, the former GM of the Mets at the time he was drafted, told MLB Network on Monday that he was disappointed to see Alonso go because baseball is an entertainment business.
Alderson, who was at the forefront of the analytical movement with the Oakland A’s, isn’t necessarily wrong. Not every entertaining player needs to be signed.
Tim Tebow didn’t need to be signed — by Alderson, no less — and there are plenty of others throughout Mets history that didn’t need to be either.
But the Mets should have done what was needed to keep a homegrown fan-favorite who proved crucial to the clubhouse and the team’s success on the field.
The Mets got lost in what they thought Alonso would be in five years and lost track of what he brings now. Baseball is played by people and watched by people.
Stearns needs a better feel for the people in the Mets’ clubhouse.
The Mets probably should have given the fans some sort of explanation as to why they didn’t even bother to try with Alonso last winter. It’s too late for that now, though.
The only thing they can do is learn from this whiff.

Gaints

John Harbaugh is using the Giants’ impressive but imperfect Sunday night win over the Dallas Cowboys
to create another potential coaching advantage against the Los Angeles Rams in Week 2.
Harbaugh is proud of his team’s 28-20 win, but the coach’s attention to detail does not allow him to overlook the corrections necessary to improve.
“We understand that how you play this week is not gonna be good enough to win next week,” Harbaugh said Monday afternoon.
“How we played [Sunday] night will not win the game next Monday night. It won’t be good enough. So we’ve got to improve.”
So as much as Jaxson Dart’s offense cooked the Dallas Cowboys’ defense in prime time, Harbaugh already has his offensive players zeroing
in on a problem they got away with in the victory: “Ball security.”
“We’re putting the ball on the ground entirely too much,” Harbaugh said on Zoom.
“It’s gotta change. We’ve gotta make the change on that.
“Every single ball carrier has got to make sure that it gets better,” he continued.
“Sometimes as coaches we gotta drill it, we gotta teach it and we gotta make sure that we do it better.
Because the ball was on the ground four times. That’s not even close to being acceptable as an example.”
Isaiah Likely, Dart, Tyrone Tracy Jr. and Cam Skattebo all fumbled. They were fortunate
that teammates recovered three of the four, excluding Tracy’s.
They won’t always be so lucky.
So although Likely had a monster game receiving, with eight catches for 78 yards and two touchdowns,
Harbaugh noted postgame that Likely “knows he’s going to get that video about three o’clock in the morning with a comment.”
The coach reaches out to players with compliments, too.
But he said communicating directly about corrections keeps the team on the same page about what kind of football Harbaugh expects, which is what matters most.
“It’s always good to make sure you continue to stay aligned in terms of the football,” Harbaugh said.
“That’s the most important thing that we need to be on the same page about is our football. All the time. Every second. Every step of the way.
“I think guys appreciate it,” he added. “A lot of times it’s really positive, too, something they did really well, just pointing out, ‘Hey, man, this is awesome. This is incredible what you did out there, and keep rolling.” Harbaugh doles out plenty of praise.
“The offensive line, they may be up for the position group of the game,” he said.
“Could be the D-line too, though. It would be between those two groups.”
Rookie right guard Francis “Sisi” Mauigoa allowed zero pressures in 36 pass blocks, per NFL NextGen Stats,
the most blocks by a rookie without a pressured allowed in Week 1 since 2018.
On defense, Jevon Holland’s interception of Dak Prescott late in the first half changed the game,
after Kayvon Thibodeaux pressured the Cowboys quarterback into a throwaway.
“For Jevon, we chased [Prescott] out of the pocket, had him on his back foot, tried to throw it out of bounds,
couldn’t quite get it there,” Harbaugh said. “And here comes Jevon Holland out of nowhere like a lightning bolt.
He has great hands, makes the play on the ball.
Now we got the ball back with a little bit of time, go down and score a touchdown with great execution.”
The Giants’ defense committed five of the Giants’ 10 penalties, though,
including four defensive pass interference calls: three on slot corner Dru Phillips and one on corner Greg Newsome.
Then Holland was hit with a fourth quarter unnecessary roughness penalty for a late hit on Cowboys receiver Ryan Flournoy.
“Penalties killed us early,” Skattebo said. “So if we clean up those penalties early, this game could have been way different.
We could have been up three touchdowns going into the fourth quarter. If we clean up penalties and continue to build off each other,
I think we’re gonna be in a good spot.”
On offense, left guard Jon Runyan Jr. was called for two false starts, right tackle Jermaine Eluemunor had one start and Dart was hit with a delay of game.
Safety Elijah Campbell also was called for holding on special teams.
Runyan and Eluemunor also were part of the offensive line that paced 165 rushing yards as an offense.
Eluemunor led the team with a 88.9% run block win rate, per ESPN. So again, Harbaugh’s Giants laid plenty of encouraging foundation for their identity, too.
Their stickler coach might have gotten the best of both worlds with his team’s opener,
though: They played well enough to win and make a statement, but they made enough mistakes that just maybe, Harbaugh can make just as large an imprint on the Giants’ improvements in Week 2.
And beat the Rams.
“We’ve got to keep improving and put everything we’ve got into this week to improve the most we possibly can if we’re gonna have the best opportunity
to win this game,” Harbaugh said. “We’ve got a lot to do. There’s so many things on that tape as a coach that you focus on that you know have to be better.
So we do have a good understanding now of where we’re at, in terms of all the details with our football.
And we understand because of that how far we have to go still. So that’s what we’ll be working on.”
